The Idoma People

The Idoma are an important ethnic group of central Nigeria, with their homeland located mainly in the southern part of present-day Benue State.

Idoma history is older than the creation of modern Benue State and the formation of Nigeria as an independent country.

Origins and Migration

Idoma origin traditions are preserved through oral history, family genealogies and community traditions.

Many Idoma communities connect their ancestral history with Apa and the wider Kwararafa region. Historical research, however, shows that the development of Idoma identity involved different groups and migrations over time.

Different communities may therefore preserve different traditions about Idoma origins.

Life and Community

Farming has historically been central to life in Idoma communities. Yams and other crops have played important roles in food production, family life and local economies.

Family relationships, community traditions, oral history, music and cultural practices have helped preserve Idoma identity across generations.

Colonial Period

British colonial rule brought significant political and administrative changes to Idoma communities during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Colonial administration also changed traditional political structures and influenced the development of recognised traditional institutions.

Preserving Our History

Idoma history continues to live through language, names, stories, family histories, music, dance, festivals, farming, traditional institutions and knowledge passed from elders to younger generations.

IDOMA HUB is created to help preserve and share this knowledge with present and future generations.
